Applications can register class-based synchronous and asyncio server and client handlers for parameterized namespace routes while retaining concrete routing context. Routed handlers coexist predictably with exact and catch-all registrations and remain visible through administrator instrumentation.

`NamespaceRoute` accepts absolute templates with literal and named segments, exposes `template` and `parameter_names`, and rejects malformed or duplicate declarations with `InvalidNamespaceRoute`, a ValueError whose `template` identifies the input. Complete-namespace matches return independent decoded parameter mappings and the concrete namespace in `NamespaceMatch` without mutating the route. Resolution prefers exact registrations, then parameterized routes ordered by literal-segment count and depth, then catch-all; indistinguishable routes raise `NamespaceRouteConflict`, a ValueError with `first_template` and `second_template`. Server and client registrations return a `NamespaceRegistration` exposing effective `route` and `active` state, and replacement or removal atomically preserves one owner while existing bindings retain their handler and new dispatches use replacements. Malformed or conflicting attempts preserve the prior active owner in routing and administrator views. Every synchronous and asynchronous server or client invocation sees only its concrete `route_params` and `route_namespace`, including through nesting, concurrency, awaits, completion, failure, and cancellation; access outside dispatch raises `LookupError`. The `server_stats` payloads from synchronous `Server.instrument()` and asynchronous `AsyncServer.instrument()` report identical precedence-ordered snapshots of active exact, parameterized, and catch-all registrations, including templates, ordered parameters, lifecycle state, conflicts, and the effective owner. Existing default namespace normalization, exact replacement, wildcard arguments, helper delegation, and legacy disconnect behavior remain unchanged.

Overlapping server routes dispatch a concrete namespace to the most specific handler with precisely its extracted parameters. Replacing a route during active nested and concurrent bindings leaves old invocations and new dispatches with their respective handlers. Failing and cancelled coroutine handlers leave later invocations with clean, newly matched bindings. Equivalent synchronous and asynchronous route sets produce matching dispatch choices and administrator ordering after active or displaced receipts are removed across server and client registries.
