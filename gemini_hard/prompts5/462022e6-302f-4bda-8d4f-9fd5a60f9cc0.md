# Julius: a saved game that drifts off course, repaired without a test suite

I load an old save and let the settlement run forward, and what grows out of it is no longer the settlement that save used to grow. Nothing about the save changed and nothing about how I play it changed, yet the same starting state now diverges in many independent ways, and in a few places the game stops outright. The repository has received 30 separate undocumented edits.

Things end up in places I never put them. A newly placed structure is recorded at the wrong spot, its column taken from its row, so it sits somewhere unrelated to where I meant it and deliveries, paths and coverage follow it there. Putting down the victory monument that a street passes through leaves that street broken, its paving laid a row away from where the monument stands. The racetrack reports the point where it meets the street at the wrong place, so whatever should appear at or head for its gate is sent elsewhere. Something loosed at a target sets off along a heading worked out from the wrong pair of coordinates and flies away from what was aimed at.

What I see drawn is wrong as often as what I see placed. A freshly sown field of crops draws its planted squares from outside the growth range whenever the growth value would fall below zero, showing garbled pictures instead of bare furrows. After I clear a patch, the bare-ground look is redrawn over the wrong span of squares, so part of what I cleared keeps the appearance it had before. Riders are drawn from the wrong band of frames entirely, showing another pose instead of the one on horseback. The patrolman who puts out fires and runs down thieves, once he comes to blows, is drawn with the same frame whichever way he faces, so his attack points one fixed way no matter who he is striking. The remains of the dead never finish fading and stay on the ground indefinitely instead of clearing and freeing the square.

Whoever should move stands still, and whoever should stop keeps going. One kind of striped grazing beast stops partway through its wandering and stands still, because the count it waits on never grows. A grazing pack takes a heading outside the range of headings once it passes the last one, so the group wanders nowhere sensible and fails to move on. A wanderer with nowhere to live that loses its way keeps walking instead of giving up, and the home set aside for it stays reserved forever, so nobody else can move in. The men who shift cargo between moored ships and the stores ashore flip to idle the moment they begin waiting, and once they have waited out the full delay they never go idle, so cargo stops moving. Once the shape of the ground changes, whether walkers can climb onto a wall is worked out from stale data, so walkers that should be able to climb up there cannot reach it.

Who is counted, who is combined and who gets sent out all come out wrong. A dwelling is credited with drink from wells and fountains that were pulled down or never finished, because the reckoning of which homes can drink counts sources that are no longer standing. When small dwellings combine into a larger one, plots that should take no part are swallowed, and the combined dwelling covers ground it should not. In a small settlement the flat happiness bonus for dwellings is withheld while in a large one it is handed out, so mood and the flow of newcomers move the wrong way. A fort asks for the wrong kind of man: a fort that is not for foot troops asks for foot troops, and forts for javelin throwers or riders never ask for their own, so the men who head out to fill a fort do not match it. The small horses that circle the racetrack are made while a race is already under way and are not made when the track is idle, and the notice that the track is working appears on the wrong occasion. A guard for a lookout post is sent out even when no post is waiting for one, producing a guard attached to nothing or a crash.

Following the goods tells the same story. A grain field fills against the wrong completion threshold, so the quantity it yields over a year is far from what the labour assigned to it should give. A loaded cart leaving a workshop still sets out to deliver a resource I have told the settlement to hold back, so goods meant to be kept keep draining out of the yard. When a cart picks which store to carry goods to, the extra cost charged against less preferred stores is ignored, so the choice falls on a store that should not win and carts walk long detours. A divine punishment that strips the largest store of grain or goods crashes the game when there is nothing eligible to be taken.

The year's accounts and the dealings with the ruler abroad no longer come out right. The sums the grandest patrician homes fail to pay each month are never gathered up, so the yearly record of money owed and not received shows nothing for them. Goods sold overseas contribute nothing to the year's recorded earnings from selling overseas, so the annual breakdown shows the selling happening with no money attached. How well off the settlement is reckoned to be moves the wrong way with respect to last year's tribute: the penalty is taken in years the tribute was paid and withheld in years it was not. The reward for beating back the army the ruler sends against me, the rise in favour and the message of respect, is granted while those troops are still on the field undefeated, and the count of days before their next arrival never advances, so the threatened attack never comes. Losing a far-off engagement because the force I sent overseas was too weak passes silently, with no message raised. A thief's haul from the treasury is left unbounded, so a single robbery can take far more money than a theft should remove.

It worked before a set of undocumented edits to the source of julius. This checkout has
no test suite.

Restore the behavior described above.

Record the functions that contain those edits in `/app/rca.json`, one entry each:

```json
{
  "causes": [
    {
      "file": "/workspace/repo/src/building/animation.c",
      "function": "enclosing_function_name"
    },
    {
      "file": "/workspace/repo/src/building/animation.c",
      "function": "Type.method_name"
    }
  ]
}
```

Each entry is an object with a `file` and a `function`; anything in `causes` that is not
an object counts as a wrong entry. `file` is the absolute path of the file, in the
repository checked out at `/workspace/repo`, written exactly as it appears in the tree.
A file inside a directory keeps its directories: the example above is
`/workspace/repo/src/building/animation.c`, not `animation.c`. The same path relative to
the repository root, such as `src/building/animation.c`, is accepted too. A path outside
`/workspace/repo`, or a path with a `..` segment, does not name a file in the repository
and counts as a wrong entry.

`function` is the function name. A class or receiver prefix, written `Type.name`, is
accepted anywhere and is required when two functions in the same file share a name, since
the bare name cannot tell them apart; a function with no class or receiver has no prefix to
add, and its bare name names it. When an edit sits inside a function nested in another
function, name the innermost function that directly contains it; the enclosing function's
name is accepted in its place, except when that function holds another edit too, where only
the innermost names tell them apart. Name every function one of the undocumented edits is
in, and only those, once each: an entry for an edit another entry already names, such as
the same function written twice, with and without its prefix, or by its enclosing function
beside its innermost one, counts as a wrong entry. Where you wrote the fix does not matter:
if you restored the behavior from a caller or from a helper of your own, name the function
the edit is in rather than the one you wrote. If you suspect a function but found no edit
in it, leave it out.
