# Dragonfly: twenty injected faults, no test suite to find them with

This library drives a sandbox mining-and-building game, and a great deal of what a player does in it now comes out wrong. Nothing about the rules of the game changed and nothing about my own code changed, yet the same actions that used to give the right result now give a different one, and in several places something that used to happen does not happen at all. The repository has received 20 separate undocumented edits. What follows is everything I have been able to observe from the outside.

Digging with a tool in hand is mistimed everywhere I look. Plain stone with a diamond pickaxe, the same stone while hovering off the ground, the same stone underwater both with and without the charm that cancels the water penalty, an ore struck with a wooden pickaxe too weak to harvest it at all, and the hurried and the sluggish potion cases alike: every single one of them reports a time that disagrees with the figure I worked out by hand, all skewed the same way. Separately, a diamond pickaxe bearing a level-three digging-speed charm now digs at exactly the pace of a bare one, so the soft red rock of the lower realm it used to clear instantly now takes measurable effort, as though that charm did not belong on a digging tool at all.

Building a gateway between realms no longer works either. A surround two wide and three tall, laid out properly in either of the two flat orientations, is not reported as complete when I ask about the square just inside it, and neither is the largest legal one; setting a fire charge against the floor inside such a surround eats the charge but leaves the interior empty. Yet a surround in which one of the pieces around it has been swapped for the weeping, purple-dripping variant of the same dark stone is cheerfully reported as complete, when that variant should never finish one. And once a gateway is lit, knocking out one of the pieces around it and letting the interior square beside the hole hear about that change fails to take the rest down: interior squares are left standing on their own. Two separate things go wrong on the way down, the check that decides whether the surround is still intact and lit, and the sweep that works out which interior squares count as one group.

Crossing from one realm to another fails from several directions at once. A gateway already built and lit in the lower realm is not found when something arrives hunting for one there, so nothing recognises the destination it was aimed at. A creature that walks into a gateway while its own movement is being processed never leaves the realm it started in: it insists a crossing is already under way when none is, and none is ever started. A thrown pearl that touches a gateway from inside such processing never finishes its trip either, because the work queued to happen once that processing completes is partly discarded, with the first queued piece silently never happening, so the receiving realm never hears of any arrival; and where there is no gateway to arrive at, the pearl is neither returned nor stamped as it should be. Anything done to a creature through a stored handle now reports the opposite outcome from what actually happened, and a panic raised inside such a piece of work does not resurface as the value thrown where it was asked for; falling sand, which ought to simply stay put, goes wrong by the same route. Stepping onto the square that leads to the outermost realm makes working out the space a mover occupies die outright with an index error, both widening that space by given amounts and shifting it somewhere else failing that way, so the mover stays where it was and the five-by-five dark platform that should be waiting on the far side is never laid. Converting a spot in the lower realm back to the surface realm scales one flat coordinate up by eight as it should but scales the other down instead of up; the conversion in the other direction, and the clamping of height at both extremes, still come out right.

Circuits built out of dust and torches misbehave in several ways. A stretch of dust handed a strength reading identical to the one it already holds rewrites itself and tells the pieces next to it about that, while a reading that genuinely differs leaves it alone; watching the topmost dust of a staircase of dust and glowstone after I place or take away a single piece one square away, its strength keeps flipping for a dozen passes and it keeps being told of changes, where before it settled at once and stayed silent. Asking a piece of dust which pieces above and below can take charge from it gives three wrong answers in a row: from dust standing on stone up to dust sitting on glowstone the upward one is missing, from dust sitting on glass the downward one is missing, and from dust sitting on glowstone the downward one is listed even though what is underneath should stop that hop. A torch that has burnt out now springs back to life after edits made three or four squares away along a line of dust, or after an unrelated piece of dust elsewhere is taken away, none of which should reach it, as though dust carrying no charge at all were being treated as carrying some. In the other direction, putting stone directly beside, above or below a burnt-out torch no longer revives it at all, and every terrain change now drags a wider span of nearby columns into its brightness recalculation than the change itself could ever reach. And a torch sitting in a loop that keeps retriggering itself never burns out any more, however far the clock is advanced, so the revival that should follow cutting the loop's dust never comes either.

One last thing, unrelated to all of that: in a realm advanced in lock-step with nothing at all watching it, stepping the clock forward three times by hand leaves a creature I have just spawned at exactly the spot it started at, not having moved at all.

It worked before a set of undocumented edits to the source of dragonfly. The repository has
no test suite to run: finding out what is wrong, and convincing yourself you have fixed
it, is part of the task.

Restore the behavior described above.

Record the functions that contain those edits in `/app/rca.json`, one entry each:

```json
{
  "causes": [
    {
      "file": "server/block/accessor.go",
      "function": "enclosing_function_name"
    }
  ]
}
```

`function` is the function name. A class prefix is ignored if you include one. When an
edit sits inside a function nested in another function, either name is accepted. Name
every function one of the undocumented edits is in, and only those. Where you wrote the
fix does not matter: if you restored the behavior from a caller or from a helper of your
own, name the function the edit is in rather than the one you wrote. If you suspect a
function but found no edit in it, leave it out.


Before you edit any code, write your research and your plan to /app/RESEARCH_AND_PLANNING.md. Cover what you learned about the existing code and the constraints that shape the change, then how you intend to carry it out and why, including how you will know it works. Be concrete - the files, names and decisions specific to this codebase, not generalities. Complete the document before you touch any code: it is the record of your up-front investigation and decisions, and it should not be rewritten to match whatever the implementation turns into afterwards. Once it is done, implement the plan.
